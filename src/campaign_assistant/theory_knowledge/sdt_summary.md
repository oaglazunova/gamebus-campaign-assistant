# Self-Determination Theory summary

Self-Determination Theory (SDT) focuses on the quality of motivation and the satisfaction of three basic psychological needs:

- autonomy: people experience choice, personal relevance, and non-controlling support;
- competence: people feel capable, receive clear feedback, and can experience progress or mastery;
- relatedness: people feel socially connected, respected, and supported.

For health behavior-change campaigns, SDT-oriented review should consider whether tasks, feedback, rewards, and progression support autonomy, competence, and relatedness.

Points, badges, levels, and rewards can be useful, but they should be framed carefully. If they feel controlling, punitive, or purely external, they may weaken autonomous motivation. If they provide meaningful feedback, acknowledge effort, and support progress, they may support competence and engagement.

The campaign export alone usually cannot prove SDT alignment. A stronger assessment requires the intervention rationale, task wording, feedback tone, degree of participant choice, and intended use of social or reward mechanisms.
