# Behavior Change Technique Taxonomy v1 summary

Behavior Change Techniques (BCTs) are observable, replicable components of behavior-change interventions. BCT Taxonomy v1 groups many common techniques used across health and behavior-change domains.

Useful high-level BCT categories for campaign review include:

- Goals and planning: goal setting, action planning, problem solving, reviewing goals.
- Feedback and monitoring: self-monitoring, feedback on behavior, feedback on outcomes.
- Social support: practical support, emotional support, social comparison.
- Shaping knowledge: instruction, information about health consequences, information about social/environmental consequences.
- Natural consequences: salience of consequences, anticipated regret, information about emotional consequences.
- Comparison of behavior: demonstration of behavior, social comparison.
- Associations: prompts/cues, habit formation, restructuring physical/social environment.
- Repetition and substitution: behavioral practice, graded tasks, behavior substitution.
- Reward and threat: material reward, social reward, self-reward, incentives.
- Regulation: reducing negative emotions, conserving mental resources.
- Self-belief: verbal persuasion, mental rehearsal, focus on past success.
- Identity: identity associated with changed behavior.

When reviewing a campaign, treat BCT identification as advisory unless the campaign explicitly labels techniques.
